## 1. Formula

```
base = 400 + (clamp(OVR, 50, 90) - 50)^2 * 6
ageBp: <=23 8500 | 24-28 10500 | 29-31 10000 | 32-34 8500 | 35+ 7000
durationBp: 1y 10800 | 2y 10200 | 3y 10000 | 4y 9600
yearOne = round10(base * ageBp * durationBp / 10000 / 10000)
yearOne = min(yearOne, maxYearOneForDuration(duration))
yearOne = clamp(yearOne, 400, 8000)
```

`maxYearOneForDuration` is 8,000 / 7,619 / 7,257 / 6,911 CU for 1–4 years so the
5% schedule never crosses the individual maximum. The estimate is based only on
public OVR, age, position and observable attributes. It deliberately does not
reuse legacy bootstrap salaries.

Acceptable range: 85–115% of the estimate, capped to the schedule maximum.
Expected role: starter OVR≥78, rotation ≥70, bench ≥62, developmental below.
Preferred duration: 2 years at ≤23, 3 at 24–28, 2 at 29–31, 1 at 32+.

## 5. Acceptance and rejection evidence

- Offer at the estimated value, user team, preferred duration: 268 acceptable,
  2 salary-too-low out of 270. The two rejections are fit/duration adjustments
  above the 100% midpoint, not hidden information.
- First acceptable salary across all 270 players: 450/1,900/2,293/6,500 CU
  (min/median/mean/max). Every player had a legal acceptable salary.
- Scanning low salaries produced 10,224 `salaryTooLow` results; no random or
  order-dependent result occurred.
- Depth test: adding two stronger same-position players changed a legal offer
  from salary-too-low to `poorFit` (reason `roleOrFranchiseFit`).
- Two-way offers are rejected for OVR≥65 or age≥27.

## 6. Financial feasibility

Across 90 team-seasons with a standard shortage:

- 89 can fill the shortage under cap and cash using the cheapest 85%-of-value
  floors.
- Shortage cost: 850/850/870/910 CU (min/median/mean/max).
- One infeasible case: seed `1604126146`, `team-23`, shortage 2, cost 910,
  cap room 600 (projected cash is positive at 22,390). This is the documented
  minimum-roster exception case, not a market failure.

This is financial feasibility, not an acquisition outcome. Role fit, player
acceptance and competition can raise the real cost. The engine does not force
teams ready and does not choose AI signings.

## 7. Anti-exploit rationale

- An 80+ OVR player requires roughly 5.9k–7.6k; a 400 CU offer is rejected.
- Salary is the dominant threshold; fit moves it by at most ±20% before duration.
- Two-way contracts cannot be used for established players.
- Longer off-preferred durations require 3% more per year difference.
- No random acceptance, no rerolls, no wall-clock or hash input.
- Evaluation is pure; repeated calls and order changes produce identical output.
- Hidden potential/trajectory changes produce identical decisions by test.

## 8. Limitations

- Values are a calibrated design convention, not observed market prices.
- No negotiation, bidding, auctions, preferences beyond age/situation/depth, or
  AI offer policy.
- Player acceptance uses public role expectations only; no promises about
  playing time or promises from the franchise.
- Financial feasibility ignores competition for the same free agents and the
  opportunity cost of other roster needs.
- One near-cap team needs the bounded minimum-roster exception; T08E does not
  implement relief.

## 9. Recommended future workflow

1. An offer UI or AI policy selects a free agent and explicit terms.
2. `FreeAgentOfferEvaluator` returns financially-illegal vs player decision.
3. Only an acceptable decision may be submitted to
   `FreeAgentAcquisitionService.acquire` (T08D).
4. Persist the resulting acquisition; never persist rejected offers or replay
   uncertain writes.
